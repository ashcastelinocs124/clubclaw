## Content Pillars

Posts should fall into one of these categories:

1. **Event Announcements** — upcoming lectures, workshops, socials, hackathons
2. **Recaps & Highlights** — what happened at a recent event, key takeaways, photos
3. **Member Spotlights** — shoutouts for MTS or members doing cool things
4. **Technical Content** — bite-sized insights from lectures (RAG, multi-agent systems, prompt engineering, etc.)
5. **Club Milestones** — membership growth, partnerships, achievements
6. **Open Source / Project Updates** — ClubClaw releases, tools the club is building

---

## Tone & Style

- Professional but approachable — sounds like a student org, not a Fortune 500 company
- Enthusiastic without being cringey — genuine excitement, not hype
- Concise — LinkedIn favors short punchy paragraphs, not walls of text
- Use line breaks between paragraphs for readability
- Emojis are fine sparingly — don't overload

---

## Post Structure

1. **Hook** — first line grabs attention (this is what shows before "see more")
2. **Body** — 2-4 short paragraphs with the actual content
3. **CTA** — what should the reader do? (join the Discord, attend the event, comment, share)
4. **Hashtags** — 3-5 relevant hashtags at the end

---

## Hashtags

Always include:
- `#AgenticAI`
- `#UIUC`

Pick 1-3 more based on the post topic:
- `#OpenSource` — for project/tool posts
- `#AIAgents` — for technical content
- `#StudentOrg` — for community/culture posts
- `#MachineLearning` `#LLMs` `#PromptEngineering` — for specific technical topics

---

## Guardrails

- **No controversial opinions** — stick to AI/tech, don't post about politics, religion, or divisive topics
- **No member personal info** — don't share emails, phone numbers, or personal details without consent
- **No bashing** — don't trash other clubs, companies, or tools
- **No misleading claims** — don't exaggerate membership numbers, partnerships, or achievements
- **No AI slop** — every post should feel like a human wrote it, not a generic AI template
- **No excessive self-promotion** — provide value, don't just say "we're amazing" repeatedly
- **Keep it student-appropriate** — professional enough that a recruiter or professor seeing it would be fine
- **Credit sources** — if referencing a paper, tool, or someone's work, tag or credit them

---

## What NOT to Post

- Internal drama or complaints
- Unverified partnerships or sponsorships
- Screenshots of private conversations
- Content from lectures without speaker permission
- Anything that could embarrass the club or university

---

## Posting Cadence

- Aim for 2-3 posts per week
- At minimum: 1 event announcement + 1 recap or technical post per week
- Don't post more than once per day — LinkedIn throttles visibility
